### Root-Cause and Precursor Categories

#### 1\. Inadequate Training and Competency

* **Description:** Operational personnel, contractors, and key supervisors lacked proper training, certifications, emergency orientation, or adequate technical knowledge of safety procedures.  
* **Applicable Cases:**  
* **Case 2 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/05):** The victim was recently approved as a topman but had no hands-on training on safety gear, height-work, hazard recognition, or emergency response 13, 14\.  
* **Case 3 (OISD/CS/2026-27/E\&P/06):** There was no structured training module to equip the electrical crew with necessary troubleshooting and maintenance skills for the AHU and compressor 15\.  
* **Case 4 (OISD/CS/2026-27/E\&P/09):** All workers performing the high-pressure gasket replacement were "unskilled" and lacked training in pressurized isolation procedures 16, 17\.  
* **Case 5 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/13):** Key personnel held valid well control certificates, but their demonstrated knowledge was not commensurate with their certificates 18, 19\. Additionally, recently transferred managers had no formal induction or orientation 20\.  
* **Case 7 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/18):** Contractual employees underwent BOSIET training that was unapproved, could not be verified online, and lacked digital QR codes 21, 22\.  
* **Case 8 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/19):** Crew members had no prior experience on the dismantling procedures of the specific rig, and operational-level safety training was not consistently conducted 23\.  
* **Case 9 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/21):** Personnel demonstrated inadequate well control competency and skills during well activity 24, 25\.

#### 2\. Deficient Supervision and Leadership

* **Description:** A complete absence or breakdown of supervisory oversight, failing to assign mentors, monitor critical tasks, or coordinate contractors.  
* **Applicable Cases:**  
* **Case 1 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/04):** OIM and authorized representatives failed to review and verify Category III inspection reports, allowing critical deficiencies to pass unnoticed 26, 27\.  
* **Case 2 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/05):** No mentor or supervisor was assigned to provide guidance to the newly approved topman 13, 14\.  
* **Case 4 (OISD/CS/2026-27/E\&P/09):** Deficient leadership and lack of supervision over highly pressurized gasket-replacement activities 17\.  
* **Case 8 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/19):** Key personnel (Installation Manager, HSE Engineer, Rig Superintendent, Rig Manager, and HSE Advisor) were simultaneously absent from the site during a critical job 23, 28\.  
* **Case 11 (OISD/CS/2024-25/PL/07):** Deep excavation in restricted conditions was carried out as an entirely unsupervised activity 29\.

#### 3\. Substandard Maintenance and Inspection Practices

* **Description:** Skipping OEM-recommended inspections, using non-standard components, and failing to perform required calibrations or integrity testing on active equipment.  
* **Applicable Cases:**  
* **Case 1 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/04):** No evidence of Category II inspections; Category III reports were missing core dimensions and checks 30-32. Additionally, the OEM-recommended latch pin was replaced with an undersized, loose 6mm cotter pin 30, 33\.  
* **Case 3 (OISD/CS/2026-27/E\&P/06):** Inadequate preventive maintenance, a lack of written OEM monthly inspection guidelines, and missing calibration records for low/high-pressure switches 34, 35\.  
* **Case 6 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/14):** Inadequate daily checklist checking of the twin stop device, allowing it to remain bypassed or unset 36, 37\.  
* **Case 7 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/18):** Railings/handrails suffered from a non-standard method of attachment and had rusted or cracked bases 22, 38\.  
* **Case 8 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/19):** No periodic inspection report was available for the ratchet boomer, which had pre-existing damage 23, 39\.  
* **Case 10 (OISD/CS/2025-26/PL/03):** Pigging and inline inspections were never conducted since commissioning due to wax buildup 40\.

#### 4\. Flawed Risk Assessment and Operational Planning

* **Description:** Preparing operational GTOs or plans that ignore offset/historical data, executing non-routine tasks without Job Safety Analysis (JSA) coverage, or operating outside safe pressure limits.  
* **Applicable Cases:**  
* **Case 2 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/05):** Dangerous oil-contaminated surfaces were not identified or addressed in the JSA or during the Toolbox Talk (TBT) 41\.  
* **Case 3 (OISD/CS/2026-27/E\&P/06):** Inadequate hazard identification and risk assessment; failing to include compressor explosion hazards in the rig risk register 35\.  
* **Case 4 (OISD/CS/2026-27/E\&P/09):** Work on a pressurized flange was performed without a Work Permit, JSA, or TBT 17, 42\.  
* **Case 5 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/13):** Well planning predicted an oil-bearing formation and omitted a Blind Shear Ram by ignoring offset well and previous workover gas-influx data 19, 43\.  
* **Case 8 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/19):** JSA did not include steps for un-securing the travelling block or safe harness anchoring locations 28, 44\.  
* **Case 9 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/21):** The workover plan assumed oil sand based on a GTO while ignoring gas shows during actual drilling and gas encounters in reference offset wells 24, 45\.  
* **Case 10 (OISD/CS/2025-26/PL/03):** The operator continued to run the pipeline above safe operating pressure for over a year after an MTM survey identified immediate repair factor (ERF \> 1\) anomalies 46, 47\.  
* **Case 11 (OISD/CS/2024-25/PL/07):** Excavation was carried out up to 3.7 meters deep without considering and mitigating hazards such as adjacent buildings, deep-rooted trees, and narrow pipeline distance 29, 48\.  
* **Case 12 (OISD/CS/2025-26/PL/16):** Assumptions regarding parallel pipeline washout safety were flawed 49, and open-cut crossings were selected without designing for adequate trench depth or pipe thickness when HDD proved unfeasible 50\.

#### 5\. Communication Breakdowns and Coordination Gaps

* **Description:** Miscommunication between deck crew and monkey board operators, signalmen and crane operators, or lack of coordination between pipeline owners and contractors.  
* **Applicable Cases:**  
* **Case 6 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/14):** Floor crew failed to communicate the placement of the tubing pin-end on the incline ramp to the topman, who was distracted and unlatched the elevator prematurely 37, 51\.  
* **Case 8 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/19):** A direct communication gap occurred between the crane operator and the signalman regarding sling tensioning 23, 39\.  
* **Case 11 (OISD/CS/2024-25/PL/07):** Mainline contractor executed mechanized excavation without informing the pipeline owner, and the owner’s permit system failed to record the authorized supervisor’s name 52, 53\. There was also a lack of post-incident coordination with local police 29\.

#### 6\. Weak Safety Culture and Non-Adherence to Procedures

* **Description:** Treating hazard reporting systems as formalities, ignoring obvious slip/fall hazards, utilizing personal mobile phones during critical operations, or bypassing task-specific PPE.  
* **Applicable Cases:**  
* **Case 1 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/04):** Unsafe rig conditions were unobserved or ignored by the crew without reporting; safety meetings only reviewed generic numbers instead of equipment safety 26, 32, 54\.  
* **Case 2 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/05):** The topman failed to engage his full-body harness double hooks to the ladder, and climbing was unsafely conducted simultaneously with tubing pull-outs 13, 55\.  
* **Case 4 (OISD/CS/2026-27/E\&P/09):** Weak safety culture with systemic non-adherence to Permit-to-Work (PTW) protocols, JSAs, and face/eye protective gear 17, 42\.  
* **Case 6 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/14):** Rig crew used mobile phones on-duty for distraction, and a non-standard rope pull was used to unlatch the elevator 36, 56\.  
* **Case 7 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/18):** Personnel chose not to wear mandatory work vests or lifejackets during their stay on the unmanned platform 22, 57\.

#### 7\. Severe Environmental and Natural Hazards

* **Description:** Riverbank erosion, heavy rainfall, flash flooding, and cloudbursts leading to hydrodynamic stress or scours, or weather hampering rescue operations.  
* **Applicable Cases:**  
* **Case 7 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/18):** Monsoon weather conditions directly hindered search and recovery (SAR) operations for the missing overboard worker 22\.  
* **Case 12 (OISD/CS/2025-26/PL/16):** Heavy downpours, cloudbursts, and flash floods triggered river path meandering, course shifts, and riverbed erosion, exerting massive hydrodynamic stress on exposed pipelines 58, 59\.

#### 8\. Mechanical Failure and Structural Defects

* **Description:** Sudden degradation or structural failure of critical equipment, latch springs, terminal connections, or handrail attachments.  
* **Applicable Cases:**  
* **Case 1 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/04):** The elevator unlatched due to a rusted and broken latch spring and an undersized, loose cotter pin 30, 32, 33\.  
* **Case 3 (OISD/CS/2026-27/E\&P/06):** Dislodgement/snapping of HVAC compressor entry terminals caused compressor oil to spray onto live terminals, resulting in an explosion 60\.  
* **Case 7 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/18):** Mezzanine deck handrails failed structurally under the worker's weight due to weak base material (rusted weld/cracked composite) 22, 38\.  
* **Case 8 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/19):** Detachment of the ratchet boomer's hook section from one side during loosening due to excessive tension or pre-existing damage 23, 39\.

#### 9\. Defunct or Absent Critical Safety Devices

* **Description:** Operating without critical barrier devices (like Blind Shear Rams), using isolated/defunct trip tanks, or working through interlock and fire water pump failures.  
* **Applicable Cases:**  
* **Case 3 (OISD/CS/2026-27/E\&P/06):** A fixed fire suppression system was absent from the workshop area, hampering firefighting in poor visibility 15, 61\.  
* **Case 5 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/13):** The BOP stack configuration lacked a Blind Shear Ram 43; the trip tank was not connected and its level alarm was defunct 62; well monitoring was absent 18; and the blind ram was closed incorrectly on wireline 19\.  
* **Case 6 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/14):** The twin stop device failed to function/engage during the runaway descent of the travelling block because it was bypassed or unset 37\.  
* **Case 9 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/21):** The BOP stack lacked a blind shear ram to shear wireline 25, 63; the trip tank was kept isolated during perforation 63; and a fire water pump was completely missing from the site 24\.  
* **Case 10 (OISD/CS/2025-26/PL/03):** Safety instrumentation systems and interlocks to trip crude oil pumps during an abrupt pressure drop malfunctioned 46\.

#### 10\. Hazardous Workspace Obstructions and Conditions

* **Description:** Physical blockages in climbing paths, materials obstructing operational visibility, or storage of highly combustible items in work areas.  
* **Applicable Cases:**  
* **Case 1 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/04):** Materials placed on the rig floor obstructed the tugger operator's line of sight 33\.  
* **Case 2 (OISD/CS/2025-26/E\&P/05):** The standpipe gooseneck and Kelly hose obstructed the monkey ladder 55, 64\. Crude oil spillage from a wet pull-out made the derrick floor and ladder slippery 14, 41\.  
* **Case 3 (OISD/CS/2026-27/E\&P/06):** Glass wool duct insulation was highly combustible 61, and an overhead platform was used as a storage area for combustible mechanical spare parts and filters 15, 35\.

#### 11\. Delayed Execution of Corrective Actions

* **Description:** Administrative or logistical delays in executing permanent engineering solutions (such as pipeline realignments or HDD rerouting) despite known and documented safety risks.  
* **Applicable Cases:**  
* **Case 10 (OISD/CS/2025-26/PL/03):** Management failed to execute immediate pressure reductions or implement corrective action plans after magnetic tomography (MTM) surveys indicated extreme anomalies and predicted failures 47, 65\.  
* **Case 12 (OISD/CS/2025-26/PL/16):** Delays in executing planned longer-length HDD realignments, despite administrative awareness of severe riverbank erosion and meandering trends, directly resulted in operations operating over vulnerable zones 50, 66\.
