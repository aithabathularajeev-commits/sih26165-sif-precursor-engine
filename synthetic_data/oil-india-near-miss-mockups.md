## CASE STUDY 1: OIL/CS/2026-27/E&P/NM/01 (SYNTHETIC)
**Title:** Near-Miss: Defective Single-Joint Elevator Latch Pin Identified During Pre-Shift Walkaround
**Location:** Onshore Drilling Rig, Moran, Assam
**Loss/Outcome:** Near-Miss (No Injury, No Asset Damage)

### BRIEF OF INCIDENT
During a routine pre-shift rig floor inspection prior to commencing tripping operations (laying down 5-1/2” drill pipe), the Shift Drilling In-Charge noticed that the secondary retention mechanism on the single-joint (SJ) elevator was compromised. The OEM-recommended 14mm safety latch pin was missing. It had been replaced by a locally fabricated 8mm soft steel wire hook. Additionally, the latch spring was noted to be heavily rusted, significantly reducing its return tension. Tripping operations were immediately halted before the elevator was engaged with any drill pipe joints, preventing a potential uncontrolled drop of pipe onto the catwalk or V-door.

### OBSERVATIONS / LAPSES
1. **Unapproved Component Substitution:** The OEM-recommended 14mm safety latch pin (secondary retention) was substituted with a non-standard, undersized (8mm) soft steel wire hook, bypassing safety requirements under API RP 8B guidelines.
2. **Substandard Maintenance:** The latch spring was rusted and showed signs of mechanical fatigue, indicating a lack of preventive maintenance and OEM storage compliance.
3. **Inadequate Rigorous Pre-Use Check:** The defect had bypassed the contractor's previous-shift daily checklist, indicating the checklist was treated as a formality rather than a rigorous physical verification.
4. **OISD Violation:** This lapse represents a direct failure to inspect contractor tools and tackles by a competent person, violating **OISD-GDN-206 Clause 7.5.1 (j)**.

### ROOT CAUSE
1. **Mechanical Precursor:** Broken/fatigued elevator latch spring and use of an unapproved, loose secondary retention wire hook in place of the OEM locking pin.
2. **Procedural Precursor:** Ineffective pre-shift inspection protocol and lack of quality assurance checks over critical lifting gear before operation.

### RECOMMENDATIONS
1. **Standardize Locking Pins:** Procure and utilize locking pins designed to remain fixed within the tool’s body, eliminating the need for separate retention wires or chains.
2. **Enforce Compliance:** Implement strict daily physical verifications of all elevators and lifting tackles by the Tool Pusher, ensuring compliance with **OISD-GDN-206 Cl. 7.5.1 (j)**.
3. **Training:** Re-train the floor crew on the hazards of utilizing non-OEM spare parts for critical load-bearing systems.

## CASE STUDY 2: OIL/CS/2026-27/E&P/NM/02 (SYNTHETIC)
**Title:** Near-Miss: Isolation of Well-Control Trip Tank Prior to Perforation Operations
**Location:** Onshore Workover Rig, Duliajan, Assam
**Loss/Outcome:** Near-Miss (No Influx, No Injury)

### BRIEF OF INCIDENT
While preparing to pull out the wireline conventional perforation gun string after successful zone perforation, the Assistant Mines Safety Officer conducted a safety walkaround. Upon inspecting the mud manifold and well monitoring systems, he observed that the well's trip tank was isolated from the wellbore. The return flow had been routed directly to the main mud tank, and the level alarm system of the trip tank was switched off. No well monitoring was actively occurring during the critical perforation-recovery sequence. The operation was suspended immediately. The manifold was re-aligned to route the well return through the trip tank, and the alarm was restored before the wireline string was pulled out, avoiding any potential swabbing-induced influx.

### OBSERVATIONS / LAPSES
1. **Active Barrier Bypass:** The trip tank was kept completely isolated from the active well system during a critical through-tubing perforation (TTP) operation. This is a severe deviation from primary well control barriers.
2. **Defunct Safety Instrumentation:** The level alarm on the trip tank was defunct/de-energized, preventing the console operator from detecting volumetric changes in the well.
3. **OISD Violation:** This isolation directly violated **OISD-STD-174 Clause 6.8 (V)**, which mandates that trip tanks must be connected and operational during well-intervention activities.
4. **Planning Lapses:** The Job Safety Analysis (JSA) had listed well monitoring as a control measure, but there was no designated personnel assigned to monitor the trip tank levels in real-time.

### ROOT CAUSE
1. **Procedural Precursor:** Operating a critical well barrier (trip tank) in an isolated state and failing to function-test the alarm system before commencing wireline perforation.
2. **Supervisory Precursor:** Lack of oversight by the Shift In-Charge to verify active BOP manifold alignments prior to wireline entry.

### RECOMMENDATIONS
1. **Interlock Alarms:** Configure well monitoring software to trigger an audio-visual alarm at the driller's console if the trip tank manifold valves are closed during well-intervention operations.
2. **Mandatory Pre-Perforation Audits:** Update the Permit-to-Work (PTW) system to require a signed manifold verification checklist from both the Operator and Company Man before wireline entry, as per **OISD-STD-174 Clause 6.8**.
3. **Competency Assessments:** Introduce regular, random well-control competency assessments for workover rig crew members.

## CASE STUDY 3: OIL/CS/2026-27/E&P/NM/03 (SYNTHETIC)
**Title:** Near-Miss: Standpipe Vibration and Kelly Hose Encroachment on Monkey Board Ladder
**Location:** Onshore Drilling Rig, Digboi, Assam
**Loss/Outcome:** Near-Miss (No Fall, No Injury)

### BRIEF OF INCIDENT
A newly approved Topman was preparing to ascend the monkey board ladder to assist in a casing run. As he reached a height of approximately 10 meters, he felt excessive mechanical vibration on the monkey ladder. He noticed that the standpipe gooseneck and Kelly hose had shifted and were rubbing directly against the ladder, slightly pinching the wire rope of his Fall Prevention Device (FPD). Following the safety-first protocol, the Topman did not attempt to force his way past the obstruction. He engaged the double hooks of his full-body harness to the nearest derrick brace, suspended his ascent, and radioed the Driller. The pump stroke rate was reduced to halt vibrations, and the Topman descended safely. The standpipe clamping brackets were later adjusted and secured to clear the ladder's path.

### OBSERVATIONS / LAPSES
1. **Physical Obstruction:** The gooseneck and Kelly hose of the standpipe partially obstructed the monkey board ladder, violating clearance norms.
2. **Structural Vibration:** Inadequate clamping on the standpipe assembly allowed high-pressure pump pulsations to transfer structural vibrations directly to the climbing ladder and FPD.
3. **OISD Violation:** Operating with an obstructed climbing path is a direct deviation from safe height-access standards outlined in **OISD-GDN-182 Clause 5.2.5 (A, iii)**.
4. **Positive Practice observed:** The Topman safely engaged his secondary double-harness hooks, demonstrating effective competency and avoiding reliance solely on the pinched FPD.

### ROOT CAUSE
1. **Physical Precursor:** Loosening of the standpipe retention clamps and poor geometric alignment of the Kelly hose relative to the monkey ladder.
2. **Inspection Precursor:** Failure to verify ladder clearance and clamp tightness during the daily rig-level safety walkaround.

### RECOMMENDATIONS
1. **Zero-Obstruction Zone:** Enforce a strict "clear zone" around all derrick ladders. Ensure that no high-pressure lines, hoses, or brackets are positioned within 1.0 meter of any climbing path.
2. **Vibration Dampening:** Install heavy-duty rubber dampening pads on all standpipe clamp assemblies.
3. **Pre-Climb Audits:** Develop a quick-reference visual poster at the base of the derrick detailing the correct alignment and clearance of derrick lines under **OISD-GDN-182**.

## CASE STUDY 4: OIL/CS/2026-27/PL/NM/04 (SYNTHETIC)
**Title:** Near-Miss: Unauthorized Mechanized Excavation Over Active Crude Oil Pipeline ROW
**Location:** Pipeline Road Crossing, Naharkatia, Assam
**Loss/Outcome:** Near-Miss (No Pipeline Strike, No Injury)

### BRIEF OF INCIDENT
An Oil India Limited line walker, conducting routine surveillance along a 12-inch crude oil trunkline Right of Way (ROW), spotted a contractor-owned JCB excavator digging a trench. The excavation was being conducted to repair a third-party telecom cable at a state highway road crossing. The excavator was operating directly within the 18-meter pipeline ROW, approximately 2.5 meters from the marked 12-inch active pipeline. The line walker immediately initiated the "Stop Work Authority." Upon investigation, it was discovered that while a general highway utility permit was active, the contractor had not informed the pipeline owner (OIL), no pipeline-specific Work Permit was issued, and no authorized OIL supervisor was present on site. The pipeline remained completely intact with no metal contact.

### OBSERVATIONS / LAPSES
1. **Permit System Breakdown:** Mechanized excavation was executed within the active pipeline ROW without an authorized Permit-to-Work (PTW) or Job Safety Analysis (JSA).
2. **Missing Supervisor Details:** The contractor’s supervisor’s name was not recorded on any joint-use permit, violating standard coordination protocols.
3. **OISD Violation:** This unauthorized work bypasses the mandatory control boundaries established in **OISD-STD-105** (Permit-to-Work Systems).
4. **Safety Culture Breakdown:** The contractor's crew was completely unaware of the physical pipeline markers and warning signs placed 20 meters away, failing to recognize the high-risk zone.

### ROOT CAUSE
1. **Procedural Precursor:** Commencing mechanized digging inside an active hydrocarbon ROW without a joint safety briefing or positive line-locating sweeps.
2. **Administrative Precursor:** Inadequate contractor induction regarding pipeline warning systems and emergency contact procedures.

### RECOMMENDATIONS
1. **Real-Time Cable Locating:** Mandate that all third-party crossings require positive hand-digging / soft-excavation checks within 5 meters of the pipeline.
2. **Public Awareness Program:** Enhance village-level and highway contractor awareness meets, distributing localized maps of pipeline corridors.
3. **PTW Enforcement:** Rigorously audit the pipeline permit system to ensure that contractor supervisor names are hard-logged and verified against active IDs before any permit release, in line with **OISD-STD-105**.

## CASE STUDY 5: OIL/CS/2026-27/PL/NM/05 (SYNTHETIC)
**Title:** Near-Miss: Air-Lock Malfunction of Corrosion Inhibitor Dosing Pump
**Location:** Crude Oil Trunkline Terminal Manifold, Duliajan, Assam
**Loss/Outcome:** Near-Miss (Zero Wall-Loss, Corrected Before Failure)

### BRIEF OF INCIDENT
During a weekly compliance audit of the corrosion control logbooks at an onshore oil transfer manifold terminal, the Asset Integrity Engineer noticed a discrepancy. The chemical consumption records for the water-soluble Corrosion Inhibitor (CI) did not correlate with pipeline throughput. A physical audit of the dosing skid revealed that the positive displacement dosing pump was running but was air-locked. The actual chemical injection rate had dropped to zero over the past 10 days. The pump was immediately bled, primed, and restored to its correct dosing rate. Ultrasonic thickness (UT) sweeps were immediately conducted across critical 45-degree bends downstream, confirming no localized wall loss had occurred during the short-term outage.

### OBSERVATIONS / LAPSES
1. **Inadequate Local Monitoring:** The daily local operator checklist marked the dosing pump as "Running" based on electrical motor status, failing to physically verify pump stroke displacement and actual chemical level drawdowns.
2. **Defunct Low-Flow Alarm:** The low-flow sensor on the chemical discharge line had been bypassed during previous calibration work and was not recommissioned.
3. **OISD Violation:** Operating with inadequate corrosion inhibition violates **OISD-STD-188 Clause 5.10**, which mandates maintaining pipeline corrosion rates strictly below 1 MPY.
4. **Maintenance Delay:** Calibration of safety-critical instrumentation on the chemical skid had been pending administrative approval for over 3 weeks.

### ROOT CAUSE
1. **Mechanical Precursor:** Air-lock inside the chemical pump head due to low levels in the supply tank during a drum changeover.
2. **Procedural Precursor:** Relying on electrical status indicators rather than positive-flow indicators or level-drop measurements to verify active barrier health.

### RECOMMENDATIONS
1. **Automated Low-Flow Interlocks:** Interlock the chemical dosing pump discharge line with the terminal control room SCADA system, triggering an instant alarm on chemical flow loss.
2. **Physical Drawdown Tests:** Mandate a weekly physical drawdown test (using calibration cylinders) to verify exact volumetric injection rates under **OISD-STD-188**.
3. **Meters & Instrumentation:** Prioritize the repair and calibration of safety-critical corrosion monitoring instrumentation, establishing strict SLA limits for bypassed sensors.
